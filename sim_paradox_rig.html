02 · Article 04 Maladaptive NMDA Homeostat & DAT Reversal

Why Stimulants Induce Paradoxical Calm While Methylphenidate Splits NE/DA

When presynaptic vesicular release efficiency \(\eta_{\text{rel}}\) is impaired, tonic \([\text{DA}]\) falls below homeostatic setpoint \(D_{\text{set}} = 65\text{ nM}\). Compensatory NMDA drive \(H_{\text{NMDA}}\) ramps up to force VTA bursting, flooding PFC/NAc with Poisson glutamate noise without fixing exocytosis. Amphetamine/Desoxyn triggers PKC-mediated DAT phosphorylation and non-vesicular reverse efflux \(J_{\text{DAT,rev}}\), restoring \([\text{DA}] \ge D_{\text{set}}\) and silencing \(H_{\text{NMDA}}\):

\[ \tau_H \frac{dH_{\text{NMDA}}}{dt} = G_{\text{homeo}}\,\max\!\left(0,\,\frac{D_{\text{set}} - [\text{DA}]}{D_{\text{set}}}\right) - H_{\text{NMDA}} \] \[ \frac{d[\text{DA}]}{dt} = k_{\text{ves}}\,\eta_{\text{rel}}\,r_{\text{VTA}}(t) + V_{\text{rev}}\,\phi_{\text{amph}} - \frac{V_{\max,\text{DAT}}[\text{DA}]}{K_m(1 + \alpha_{\text{mph}} + \alpha_{\text{amph}}) + [\text{DA}]} - k_{\text{clear}}[\text{DA}] \]
03 · Arnsten’s Prefrontal Adrenoceptor Switch (Penrose Essay)

High-Affinity \(\alpha_{2A}\) HCN Closure vs. Stress-Induced \(\alpha_{1A}\) Shunting

Moderate norepinephrine (\(\sim 45\text{–}110\text{ nM}\)) occupies high-affinity \(\alpha_{2A}\) receptors (\(\text{G}_i \downarrow \text{cAMP}\)), closing dendritic HCN (\(I_h\)) leak channels and locking persistent Layer III working-memory delay activity. Excessive NE from status-intimidation stress or unbalanced NET blockade engages lower-affinity \(\alpha_{1A}/\beta_1\) receptors (\(\text{PKC}/\text{cAMP}\)), opening spine leak channels and collapsing cortical SNR:

\[ \theta_{\alpha 2A} = \frac{[\text{NE}]^{1.4}}{45^{1.4} + [\text{NE}]^{1.4}}, \qquad \theta_{\alpha 1A} = \frac{[\text{NE}]^{2.2}}{155^{2.2} + [\text{NE}]^{2.2}}, \qquad w_{\text{rec}} = w_0 \frac{0.35 + 0.85\,\theta_{\alpha 2A}}{1 + 2.6\,\theta_{\alpha 1A} + 1.9\,B_{\text{GABA}}} \]
04 · Operational Striatal D2 Receptor Theory & Akathisia

Haloperidol Silent Antagonism vs. Aripiprazole 28% Intrinsic Ceiling

Striatal indirect-pathway D2 ("No-Go") MSNs are tonically restrained by endogenous D2 signaling (\(E_{\text{D2}}\)). Silent blockade (\(\varepsilon_{\text{hal}} = 0\)) or ultra-high-affinity partial agonism (\(\varepsilon_{\text{ari}} = 0.28,\,K_i = 0.4\text{ nM}\)) drops \(E_{\text{D2}}\) below the striatal gating threshold while disinhibiting glutamatergic/noradrenergic restlessness:

\[ E_{\text{D2}} = \frac{1.0\,\frac{[\text{DA}]}{K_{\text{DA}}} + 0.28\,\frac{[\text{Ari}]}{K_{\text{Ari}}} + 0.0\,\frac{[\text{Hal}]}{K_{\text{Hal}}}}{1 + \frac{[\text{DA}]}{K_{\text{DA}}} + \frac{[\text{Ari}]}{K_{\text{Ari}}} + \frac{[\text{Hal}]}{K_{\text{Hal}}}} \]
05 · Dorsal Raphe 5-HT1A Autoreceptor & 5-HT2C Dopamine Clamp

Acute Week-1 SSRI Firing Drop vs. Week-3 Desensitization

Acute SERT blockade raises somatodendritic raphe \([5\text{-HT}]\), activating \(5\text{-HT}_{1A}\) GIRK autoreceptors that paradoxically suppress raphe firing in Week 1 until chronic receptor desensitization restores spike output—while terminal \(5\text{-HT}_{2C}\) activation on VTA GABA interneurons dampens mesolimbic dopamine tone.
